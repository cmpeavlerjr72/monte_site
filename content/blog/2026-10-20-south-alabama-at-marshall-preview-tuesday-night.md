South Alabama scores on everybody. South Alabama gets scored on by everybody. A Tuesday night in Huntington between two teams sitting in the bottom third of SP+ is going to be a blast.

The Jaguars are 7th in FBS in scoring and dead last, 138th of 138, in defensive EPA per play. They're unbeaten in Sun Belt play anyway, which tells you how this offense has been bailing out that defense. Marshall is still looking for its first conference win and walks in off a home loss where the starting quarterback threw three picks and got pulled.

Kickoff is 7:30 ET at Joan C. Edwards Stadium. Clear the evening, it's a midweek Sun Belt game and those never end quietly.

## South Alabama: All Gas, No Brakes

::photo{src="/blog/south-alabama-at-marshall-preview-tuesday-night/card-k2.png" alt="South Alabama vs. Marshall, Season So Far" caption="South Alabama vs. Marshall, Season So Far" credit="MVPeav graphic"}

The Jaguars put up **38.8 a game**, and they do it on the ground. South Alabama runs for 220.3 yards a game, 11th in FBS, and the two wins that carry the record were a 41-36 over Ohio and a 56-49 over Arkansas State. Neither of those teams is anyone's idea of a defense, but 56 is 56.

Then you flip to the other side of the ball and it's a crime scene. South Alabama gives up 34.5 a game and 5.8 yards a carry, and by EPA per play allowed it is the worst defense in the country, 138th of 138. The success rate allowed is 51%, meaning more than half the plays run against this group are a win for the offense.

So how is this team 4-2 and clean in the league? Turnovers. South Alabama is +8 in turnover margin, 7th in FBS, and they've been living on it. When you can't stop anybody, the only way to win is to take the ball away and let the offense keep the score climbing. The losses, 24-28 to Tulane and 21-45 to Kentucky, are what it looks like when the takeaways don't show up.

::getty{id="2294973281" et="HfWebdzVSFFdA4wK277hdw" sig="K880zKf2cwB5buI1YTC7liSvcQ16xaGnCwq4zFzHMKU=" w="594" h="396" caption="Running back Keenan Phillips #3 of the South Alabama Jaguars is defended by defensive back Josh Sims #6 of the Tulane Green Wave during the second half of a gam" credit="Tyler Kaufman/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/2294973281"}

In my sims the offense runs through Jared Hollins at a median 224 passing yards, with PJ Martin at 52 rushing yards and Keenan Phillips at 42 behind him. It's a committee on the ground and the committee works.

## Marshall: Who's Throwing It, and Who's Catching It?

::getty{id="1229283680" et="LaggU9y1ThtLvLkUoPyisg" sig="V9I81XyYdOKBLJOLawAqFK8sNtPhmJZqieeC3OpnEYs=" w="594" h="396" caption="Marshall LB Tavante Beckett at Joan C. Edwards Stadium (file photo)" credit="Icon Sportswire/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/1229283680"}

Marshall is 3-3, and the two wins were squeakers over Missouri State, 30-24, and Middle Tennessee, 28-26. The losses were a 45-0 at Penn State, a 45-17 to James Madison, and the one that stings, a 27-21 home loss to Coastal Carolina.

That Coastal game is the whole story for the Herd. Carlos Del Rio-Wilson went 2-of-6 for 37 yards with three interceptions and got pulled. Zion Turner came in and threw two touchdowns. Tony Gibson said afterward that no job is secure, and I'm not going to tell you who trots out first on Tuesday because he hasn't told anyone either.

The ball security problem isn't a one-game thing. Marshall sits at **-9** in turnover margin, 133rd in FBS, and that is the number to stare at when you remember who's coming to town. South Alabama lives on takeaways. Marshall hands them out.

The offense scores 22.0 a game, 111th in FBS, so it isn't built to win a shootout. My sims run Del Rio-Wilson at a median 212 passing yards and TJ Lester at 48 on the ground, a fine night for a team that has to keep the game close and boring and nowhere near enough if it becomes an Arkansas State special.

## The Whole Game: South Alabama's Run Game vs Marshall's Run D

::photo{src="/blog/south-alabama-at-marshall-preview-tuesday-night/card-k4.png" alt="What Winning Looks Like in the Sims" caption="What Winning Looks Like in the Sims" credit="MVPeav graphic"}

South Alabama runs for 220.3 a game. Marshall allows 193.8 a game at 5.5 a carry, 128th in FBS. If you're a Herd fan you already know where this is going.

My sims agree it's the ballgame. When South Alabama wins, it rushes for 204. When it loses, 145. Same story on the other sideline: Marshall runs for 220 in the sims it wins and 151 in the ones it loses.

Two bad run defenses on the same field will do that. Whoever gets the ground game going gets to keep the other offense standing on the sideline, and against South Alabama's defense, running the clock is the only way Marshall keeps the score in the 20s.

The matchup to watch is Marshall's front seven in the first quarter. If Martin and Phillips are getting 6 a pop early, the Jaguars will have the Herd offense chasing, and a chasing Marshall offense is the one that threw three picks against Coastal.

## How My Sims See It

::photo{src="/blog/south-alabama-at-marshall-preview-tuesday-night/margin.png" alt="Marshall margin across all 10,000 sims: South Alabama wins 39%, Marshall 61%, median margin Marshall by 4" caption="Every one of the 10,000 sims: South Alabama wins 39%, Marshall 61%; the median margin is Marshall by 4" own="1"}

Marshall comes out ahead in **61%** of 10,000 sims, and the typical one ends Marshall 30, South Alabama 24. Half of those sims sit between a Marshall loss by 7 and a Marshall win by 14, so I'd call it a lean and leave it there.

What separates the Marshall wins from the losses is the thing you'd guess. In the sims Marshall wins, it turns the ball over 0.7 times. In the sims South Alabama wins, 1.5. One extra giveaway against a team that's +8 on the year is the difference between a comfortable home win and a Coastal rerun.

The other piece is possession. Marshall gets about 4.5 red-zone trips in its wins and 3.3 in its losses, and it gets there by running the ball and keeping the clock moving. The version where South Alabama wins looks like the Ohio game: the Jaguars ripping off chunk runs, the Herd forced to throw to keep up, and the throw going the wrong way.

## Bottom Line: Two Bottom-Third Teams, One Home Field

::getty{id="460060504" et="apJtXrlaSXd_qviheaZVoQ" sig="IA8U8FSIJZ9yt_hYCc_ofg4i2C3X45gDOmOAsQU3oyQ=" w="594" h="396" caption="Marshall fans carry the goal posts out of Joan C. Edwards Stadium (2014)" credit="Maddie Meyer/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/460060504"}

South Alabama sits 116th in SP+ and Marshall 121st, so nobody is confusing this for a quality matchup. What tips it is the home field and the fact that South Alabama's defense can't stop a run game that's otherwise pretty ordinary. Marshall doesn't need to be good on Tuesday, it needs to be careful.

The Jaguars have already scored 56 in a conference game this year, and the Herd quarterback room is a coin flip. If the turnovers show up again, the 61% means nothing.

I'm leaning Marshall, and the longer I look at it the harder that lean gets to resist. I'll be on the couch for this one, and I'm expecting a good time. Two defenses this bad on the same field on a Tuesday night is exactly the game I started running sims to understand in the first place.
