UCLA is 10-1, sitting on the best defense in the country, and my sims still give USC 71% to walk into their building and ruin the whole season. Seventy-one. Against a team that has lost once.

ESPN's FPI has USC at 48.8%, a coin flip, so this is the biggest split between my sims and the rest of the world on the entire board. Five more underdogs are right behind it, and every one of them has a matchup reason, so I'm going to lay them out.

Underdog here means the team the rankings or FPI expect to lose. The question in each one is simple. What does the favorite do well, and does the dog have the exact thing that breaks it?

## USC Has the Arm to Crack the Best Defense in the Country

::getty{id="2306348188" et="pwcRXtQ3TDB5C5pXJxTISw" sig="RjQq2mGU2QzTGbaUYLuZ0DVrQVXYw6IP03RT0nof0HY=" w="594" h="396" caption="USC Trojans quarterback Jayden Maiava (14) passes the ball to running back Waymond Jordan (2) during the college football game between the USC Trojans and Maryl" credit="Icon Sportswire/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/2306348188"}

UCLA's defense is 1st in FBS in EPA per play and 3rd in yards allowed at 260.8 a game. My sims respect it plenty. USC goes three-and-out on 12.4% of its drives this season, and against the Bruins the sims nearly double that to 21.9%.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125111946.png" alt="Split card in USC cardinal and UCLA blue: USC wins 71% of 10,000 sims to UCLA's 29% (ESPN FPI has USC 48.8%, UCLA 51.2%), median sim score 30-20 USC, season 3-and-out rate 12.4% vs 20.3%, sim 3-and-out rate 21.9% vs 26.3%, turnover margin USC -1 vs UCLA +14." caption="USC at UCLA: the sims back the Trojans 71% of the time even though UCLA wins the turnover battle by a mile." own="1"}

So why does USC still win 71 of every 100? Because the Trojans throw it as well as anybody, 290.8 passing yards a game, 9th in FBS, with the 8th-best success rate in the country. That passing game holds up just enough, even against a UCLA pass defense giving up 172.4 a game and 5.7 a throw.

The other half is UCLA's offense, which already stalls on 20.3% of its drives and stalls on 26.3% of them in the sims against USC. The typical sim lands 30-20 Trojans, and half of them run from UCLA by 3 to USC by 19. USC has already gone and beaten Ohio State 30-18 and Penn State 31-23 on the road, so the arm is proven against grown-up defenses.

UCLA's answer is that it never beats itself. Plus-14 in turnover margin, 3rd in FBS, while USC sits at minus-1. The Bruins have won 17-15 at Oregon and 17-7 over Wisconsin and lost 16-18 at Michigan, so they live in the exact kind of game where a tipped ball decides it.

One more wrinkle. Ty Dieffenbach left the Michigan loss with a hip injury, X-rays were clean, and his coach said he's "nowhere near in the clear" for Saturday. If he can't go, it's Madden Iamaleava, with Nico Iamaleava already done for the year with a torn ACL. My sims run on season averages and don't adjust for any of that, in either direction.

## Oregon State Gets Another Crack at the Team That Beat It by 16

Oregon State leads all of FBS in passing at 355.5 yards a game. Fresno State's pass defense is 119th, giving up 250.3. My sims put the Beavers at 69%, FPI says 48.8%, and I think the sims have the better of it because of how badly this matchup leans one way.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125111940.png" alt="Split card in Oregon State orange and Fresno State navy: Oregon State wins 69% of sims to Fresno State's 31%, ESPN FPI has it 48.8% to 51.2%, the median sim score is 27-20 Oregon State, and Fresno State won the first meeting 38-22." caption="Round Two: the sims like Oregon State, ESPN FPI and the first meeting like Fresno State." own="1"}

Fresno's defense is 112th in yards allowed at 411.8 a game and 99th in defensive success rate at 44%. Oregon State's offense has the 4th-best success rate in the country. Every down is a down the Beavers are probably winning.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125111944.png" alt="Split bar graphic: Oregon State's passing offense ranks #1 in FBS at 355.5 yards per game, while Fresno State's pass defense ranks #119 of 138, allowing 250.3 yards per game." caption="Oregon State throws for more than anyone in FBS, and Fresno State's pass defense is 119th." own="1"}

The sims actually chop Oregon State's passing down to about 260 yards in this one, and it still doesn't matter. The typical sim finishes 27-20 Oregon State, with half of them landing between Fresno by 3 and the Beavers by 17.

Fresno State has receipts, though. It beat this exact Oregon State team 38-22 earlier this season and sits at plus-8 in turnover margin, 12th in FBS. A defense that bends all day and still takes the ball away twice is how 69% turns into a long bus ride home.

## Virginia Tech's Passing Game Gets Room to Breathe Against Virginia

Virginia Tech's offense gets sacked 3.1 times a game, which is a problem for a team that wants to throw. The sims cut that to about 2.0 against Virginia, whose defense only gets home about 2 times a game itself. That extra second per drop-back is the whole reason the Hokies win 65 of every 100 sims while FPI has them at 47.9%.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125111947.png" alt="Split card in Virginia Tech maroon and Virginia orange: Virginia Tech wins 65% of sims to Virginia's 35% with a 26-20 median score, while ESPN FPI has Virginia 52.1% to 47.9% and Virginia leads turnover margin +8 to -6." caption="The sims like the Hokies; FPI and the turnover sheet like the Cavaliers." own="1"}

Give VT time and it has a passing game to use, 273.1 yards a game, 19th in FBS. Virginia's offense, meanwhile, is 109th in explosive-play rate at 11%, so the Cavaliers have to grind out drives against a defense that's fresh instead of hitting over the top of it.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1126121754.png" alt="Split card: Virginia Tech takes 3.1 sacks per game in 2026, but about 2.0 against Virginia in 10,000 sims, 1.1 fewer; Virginia's defense averages 2.0 sacks a game." caption="The sims give Virginia Tech's quarterback more time against Virginia: about 2.0 sacks, down from 3.1." own="1"}

The typical sim lands Virginia Tech 26, Virginia 20, with half of them between Virginia by 4 and the Hokies by 17.

FPI has reasons to doubt it, and they're real. Virginia Tech's defense is 126th in explosive plays allowed and 112th in pass yards allowed at 230.7 a game. Then the turnovers, Virginia at plus-8 (18th) and VT at minus-6 (112th). A team that gives it away and gives up bombs is a team that can lose to a boring offense, which is exactly what Virginia is.

## Colorado Is Dead Last in Turnovers and the Sims Bet That Stops

Colorado is 4-7 and 138th of 138 in turnover margin at minus-13. Dead last in the whole sport. It throws 1.5 interceptions a game. And my sims make this team the favorite at 64%, with FPI at 47.7%, because the sims expect the giveaways to finally slow down against UCF.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1126121751.png" alt="Colorado turns it over 2.1 times a game and UCF's defense takes it away 1.6 times a game, but in 10,000 sims Colorado's turnovers drop to about 1.2 against UCF; Colorado's turnover margin is -13, 138th of 138." caption="Dead last in turnover margin, but the sims expect Colorado's giveaways to slow down against UCF." own="1"}

Colorado turns it over 2.1 times a game. UCF's defense takes it away about 1.6 times a game, and in the sims Colorado's number drops to about 1.2. UCF's pass defense is 114th in FBS at 230.9 yards allowed, so the throws that have been getting picked all year get to be completions instead.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125112005.png" alt="Split card for UCF at Colorado: Colorado wins 64% of sims to UCF's 36% with a 27-22 median sim score, while ESPN FPI favors UCF 52.3% to 47.7%; records are UCF 6-5, Colorado 4-7." caption="The sims like 4-7 Colorado at home; ESPN FPI leans UCF." own="1"}

That still leaves a Colorado offense that's 116th in scoring at 22.5 a game. The typical sim is only 27-22 Buffs, and half of them run from UCF by 5 to Colorado by 16.

UCF's case is a hot quarterback and a bad Colorado defense. Alonza Barnett III is back from a hamstring injury and has thrown for 250-plus in three straight games, including 255 and four touchdowns against Iowa State. Colorado's defense has allowed 20-plus points in 10 straight games. UCF has already beaten BYU 39-31 and Kansas 33-32 and clinched a bowl; Colorado isn't going to one. A team with nothing to play for fixing its turnover problem in Week 13 is a big ask (tho the sims don't know what week it is).

## Arizona State Turns the Territorial Cup Into a Coin Flip

FPI gives Arizona State 22% at No. 16 Arizona. My sims say 49%, and the middle sim is a 24-24 tie. Half the sims finish within 10 points either way. That is the largest gap on the board, and it comes down to one thing. Arizona cannot run the ball.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1126121812.png" alt="Split card in Arizona red and Arizona State gold: Arizona's season average in the sim file is 343.3 pass yards per game and 9.5 yards per attempt, and the sims against ASU drop that to 262.9 and 7.3." caption="Arizona can't run, so it throws. My sims say ASU's defense takes more than two yards off every attempt." own="1"}

The Wildcats are 116th in rushing at 122.0 yards a game and 3.7 a carry, 118th. ASU's run defense is 26th in FBS, allowing 118.5. So Arizona has to throw, and it throws well, 324.4 yards a game, 4th in FBS.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125111951.png" alt="Split win-probability bars for Arizona State at No. 16 Arizona: MVPeav's sims give ASU 49% (Arizona 51%) while ESPN FPI gives ASU 22.0% (Arizona 78.0%), and the middle sim score is a 24-24 tie." caption="ESPN FPI makes Arizona a heavy favorite. My sims call the Territorial Cup a coin flip." own="1"}

My sims think ASU's defense cuts that way down. Arizona averages 9.5 yards per attempt in the sim file and the sims drop it to 7.3 against the Sun Devils. Take two-plus yards off every throw from a team that can't run and you've got a one-dimensional offense playing a one-score game.

Arizona's résumé says the same thing. Its three losses came by 11, 8 and 1 (39-40 to Utah), and it's 0-3 against Quad 1 opponents. Rank it 16th if you want, but it hasn't beaten anybody good yet (and I'm not sure a close loss to Utah proves much either way).

## Florida State Is Playing for a Bowl and Its Own Turnover Problem

::getty{id="2306282344" et="sARgbk_ARDtExXDPosDgrA" sig="AfNd3WeMXEsu6mMEqfZaloFl1dXEVjmnzIMK7ITo2hg=" w="594" h="446" caption="Quarterback Ashton Daniels #14 of the Florida State Seminoles passes the ball during the first half of the game against the NC State Wolfpack at Doak S. Campbel" credit="Don Juan Moore/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/2306282344"}

Florida State is minus-12 in turnover margin, 135th of 138 in FBS, and throws 1.3 interceptions a game. My sims give the Seminoles 42% against No. 20 Florida, FPI says 34.3%, and the whole gap is the sims expecting FSU to throw about 0.8 picks instead of 1.3. Florida's defense only takes away 0.4 a game, so the sims aren't asking for a miracle.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1126121922.png" alt="Florida State throws 1.3 interceptions a game this season (turnover margin -12, 135th of 138), but 10,000 sims against a Florida defense that forces 0.4 a game expect about 0.8, and they give FSU a 42% win chance vs ESPN FPI's 34.3% with a typical score of Florida 30, FSU 27." caption="FSU throws 1.3 picks a game. Against Florida, my sims expect about 0.8, and that is the whole gap between my 42% and FPI's 34.3%." own="1"}

Florida still wins more of them because FSU can't stop the run. The Seminoles allow 172.6 rushing yards a game, 102nd, and 4.5 a carry, 103rd. The sims hand Florida 5.0 a carry, and 5.0 a carry on a Saturday in late November is how you bleed a clock. The typical sim is Florida 30-27, with half of them between the Gators by 13 and FSU by 7.

Florida is 7-4 and a strange team to read. It beat Texas 27-24 on the road and hung 52 on Ole Miss, then lost 17-45 at Missouri and 7-23 to Oklahoma at home. The good version wins this comfortably. The bad version shows up often enough that 42% feels right to me.

The stakes are what make it a watch. Florida has knocked FSU out of a bowl twice in the last five years by winning this game, and FSU's bowl hopes are on the line again. Nothing says Tallahassee like your rival holding the keys to your December.

## The One Upset My Sims Like Most

Line them all up. USC 71%, Oregon State 69%, Virginia Tech 65%, Colorado 64%, Arizona State 49%, Florida State 42%.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-13-usc-ucla-sims/gfx-1125112028.png" alt="Logo ladder of Week 13 underdogs by sim win percentage, each against ESPN FPI: USC 71% at UCLA (FPI 48.8%), Oregon State 69% (48.8%), Virginia Tech 65% (47.9%), Colorado 64% (47.7%), Arizona State 49% (22.0%), Florida State 42% (34.3%)." caption="Four of the six Week 13 underdogs win most of my 10,000 sims. ESPN FPI has all six below a coin flip." own="1"}

Four of the six are outright favorites in my sims, which is the strangest-looking part of this list until you see the mechanism behind each one. USC has the passing game to survive the best defense in the country. Oregon State's passing game meets a secondary that's 119th. Virginia Tech gets to throw without a lineman in its face. Colorado gets a pass defense soft enough to turn interceptions into completions.

USC at UCLA is the one I keep coming back to. A 10-1 team with the 1st-ranked defense in FBS, and the sims have it losing 30-20 to its crosstown rival in the typical run. If Dieffenbach can't go, every bit of that gets worse for the Bruins. Keep an eye on that injury report, then watch the Trojans throw.
