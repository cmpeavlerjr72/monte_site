The first game in this series ended with a 33-year-old Army surgeon scoring touchdowns for Tech and Georgia fans chasing the visitors back to their train. That was 1893. Nobody has cooled off since.

119 games later the two schools can't even agree on the record. Georgia says 73-39-5. Tech says 73-41-5. Two wins from the 1940s sit in the gap, and neither side is giving an inch.

I'm a Tech fan, so I'll say up front that the recent chapter of this thing has been miserable for my side. Eight straight losses, an 8-overtime gut punch, and a "home game" last year that looked like a Georgia pep rally. We'll get to all of it. The first 70 years are a lot more fun to tell from Atlanta.

## Rocks, a Train Station and a 33-Year-Old Ringer

Georgia Tech's first football win in school history came in Athens on Nov. 4, 1893, 28-6 over Georgia. Starting your program by walking into your rival's town and taking one off them is a pretty good preview of how the next century was going to feel.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/1893-georgia-bulldogs-football-team.jpg" alt="1893 Georgia Bulldogs football team" caption="1893 Georgia Bulldogs team photo" credit="Unknown author" license="Public domain" licenseurl="" source="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1893_Georgia_Bulldogs_football_team.jpg"}

The star of that game was Leonard Wood, a 33-year-old Army post surgeon who enrolled at Tech two days before kickoff. He scored Tech's first touchdown ever against Georgia. A 33-year-old doctor signing up for classes 48 hours before the game and scoring in it is the most Georgia Tech thing that has ever happened, and it happened in game one.

Georgia's fans handled the loss about how you'd expect. Tech's own timeline says they threw rocks at the visitors and chased them back to their train. I guess UGA fans have always been obnoxious. One game in and the home crowd is already running the other team out of town.

Then came the part that actually broke things. Georgia dropped football during World War I. Tech kept playing. When Georgia came back in 1919, its parade floats read "UGA in Argonne" and "TECH in Atlanta," a joke that lands great until the other school stops speaking to you. Tech cut athletic ties on the spot. The two did bump into each other at the 1921 SIAA basketball tournament, but the football teams didn't line up against each other again until 1925.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195345.png" alt="Left-to-right timeline of how the Georgia Tech-Georgia hate started: Leonard Wood, 33, enrolls at Tech on Nov 2, 1893; Tech wins 28-6 in Athens on Nov 4, 1893; Georgia's 1919 'TECH in Atlanta' parade floats; Tech cuts athletic ties 1919-1925; Cromartie's 1977 book credited with the nickname." caption="From a 33-year-old ringer to a parade float to a nickname: how Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate got started." own="1"}

Some of the bad blood is even pettier than a parade float. Georgia dropped old gold from its colors because it looked too much like yellow, so Tech picked it up. These two have been fighting over a shade of paint for longer than either has had a stadium worth bragging about.

The name came much later. Most of the credit for "Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate" goes to author Bill Cromartie and his 1977 book on the rivalry, though not every source hands it to him.

## When Bobby Dodd Owned the State

For the first 70 years this was a coin flip, and the coin kept landing on Atlanta.

Bobby Dodd's teams won 8 straight against Georgia from 1949 to 1956. Eight. Any Georgia fan who wants to talk about streaks this week should know Tech wrote the first one, and wrote it against them.

The 1952 team won a national title in the middle of that run, Tech's third behind 1917 and 1928. Georgia had one at the time, 1942. For a long stretch of the 20th century the football school in this state was the engineering school, and Athens had to live with it.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/1952-georgia-tech-yellow-jackets-football-team.jpg" alt="1952 Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets football team" caption="1952 Georgia Tech varsity roster" credit="Unknown author" license="Public domain" licenseurl="" source="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1952_Georgia_Tech_Yellow_Jackets_football_team.png"}

When Vince Dooley took the Georgia job in 1964, Tech led the series 27-26-5. Seventy years of football and the whole thing came down to one game. If you'd told either fanbase then that the next six decades would be a landslide, both would have assumed it was going Tech's way.

## The Asterisks Georgia Still Won't Erase

Now the part where two schools full of smart people can't do arithmetic together.

During World War II Tech housed a Navy training center, and the 1943 and 1944 Tech teams beat Georgia. In 1949 UGA's Dan Magill put asterisks on both wins in Georgia's records, the argument being that Tech was fielding Navy trainees. Tech counts both and has no plans to stop.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195351.png" alt="Two scoreboards: Georgia's count of the Georgia vs. Georgia Tech series is 73-39-5 with Tech's 1943 and 1944 wins asterisked, Tech's count is 73-41-5; the asterisked 1943 game was Tech 48, Georgia 0, the biggest margin in the series." caption="Georgia leads either way, but it still won't count Tech's 1943 and 1944 wartime wins, including the 48-0." own="1"}

One of those games is the 48-0 in 1943, still the biggest margin in the series. So Georgia's official position is that the worst beating anyone has ever taken in this rivalry doesn't count. Convenient.

So the record reads two ways. Wikipedia, Covers and BVM Sports have Georgia up 73-41-5 over 119 meetings. Yahoo Sports gives 73-39-5. Georgia leads comfortably either way, which somehow makes the fight over two games from the 1940s funnier. A program sitting on a lead like that and still refusing to count a 48-0 loss is a program that has never once been over this rivalry. I kind of respect it.

## Tech Walks Out of the SEC and Dooley Walks In

Everything in this series hinges on one year.

On Jan. 24, 1964, Georgia Tech announced it was leaving the SEC, the end of Dodd's fight over the "140 Rule," and went off to play as an independent. That same year Georgia hired Vince Dooley. Tech lost its conference and Georgia found its coach in the same calendar, and the series has never looked the same.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195357.png" alt="Left-to-right timeline of the Georgia Tech-Georgia series: Dodd's Tech wins 8 straight (1949-1956), Tech announces it is leaving the SEC on Jan 24, 1964, Tech leads 27-26-5 when Dooley arrives in 1964, then Georgia goes 19-6 vs Tech under Dooley (3-0 vs Dodd) and 42-14 since 1969 per Wikipedia." caption="1964 split the rivalry in two: Tech left the SEC, Georgia hired Dooley, and the series never looked the same." own="1"}

Dooley went 19-6 against Tech, including 3-0 against Dodd himself. Tech came into his tenure leading the series by a game and left it buried.

Wikipedia has Georgia at 42-14 in the series since 1969, and that figure may not even count the latest games. Either way, 70 years of a coin flip turned into 55 years of Georgia winning nearly everything, and from the Tech side you can draw a straight line from that January announcement to every November since.

## The Games You Still Bring Up at the Bar

Six games, oldest to newest. Four of them are Tech wins, which tells you whose highlights are rarer and therefore better.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195409.png" alt="A left-to-right timeline of six Georgia Tech-Georgia games, each tile colored for the winner: Georgia 38-20 in 1980 (Walker 205 yds), Tech 40-23 in 1990 (37 straight points), Tech 51-48 in OT in 1999 (Hamilton 438 yds), Tech 45-42 in 2008 (410 rush yds), Tech 30-24 in OT in 2014 (Butker 53-yd FG) an" caption="Six games still argued over at the bar. Tech won four of them." own="1"}

**1980.** Freshman Herschel Walker carried 25 times for 205 yards in a 38-20 Georgia win in Athens and broke Tony Dorsett's freshman rushing record doing it. Georgia went on to win the national title. I'd love to tell you Tech had an answer for him. Tech did not have an answer for him.

**1990.** No. 2 Tech came into Athens on Dec. 1, fell behind 9-0, then scored 37 unanswered points and won 40-23. Tech finished 11-0-1 and took the UPI coaches' title. Hanging 37 straight on Georgia in their stadium on the way to a national championship is the best day in the series from where I'm standing.

**1999.** Tech 51, Georgia 48, in overtime at Bobby Dodd Stadium. Georgia fans still think they won this game, and they have a case. Officials ruled Jasper Sanks fumbled at the goal line in regulation; replays showed his knees were down. The SEC suspended the crew afterward, and the league's officiating supervisor, Bobby Gaston, said "It looks like they had a miss." Thanks, Bobby.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195352.png" alt="Quote card in Georgia red and Georgia Tech gold with both logos: ”It looks like they had a miss.” — SEC officiating supervisor Bobby Gaston on the 1999 Jasper Sanks fumble call (ESPN, Dec 1 1999)." caption="The SEC's officiating supervisor, a few days after the 1999 Sanks fumble call." own="1"}

What gets lost in the knees is Joe Hamilton, who passed and ran for 438 yards and 4 touchdowns that day, and Luke Manget hitting a 38-yard field goal in overtime to end it. Georgia remembers the fumble. I remember the 438.

**2008.** Paul Johnson's first year, No. 18 Tech down 16 at halftime in Athens, and then the option ate Georgia alive for two quarters. Tech ran for 410 yards, completed exactly one pass, and won 45-42. Roddy Jones carried 13 times for 214 yards. It also ended Mark Richt's 7-0 start against Tech, which had gotten very hard to hear about.

**2014.** Harrison Butker hit a 53-yarder on the last play of regulation in Athens to force overtime, and D.J. White's interception sealed it, 30-24 Tech. A kicker and a cornerback stealing a game in Sanford Stadium is exactly how Tech is supposed to win this thing.

**2024.** Georgia 44, Georgia Tech 42, in 8 overtimes at Sanford Stadium, on a Friday night that ran past midnight. Tech led 17-0 at halftime. Carson Beck threw 5 touchdown passes to drag Georgia back. Haynes King passed for 303 yards and ran for 110 and still went home with a loss. It finished one overtime short of the FBS record, the 9-OT Illinois-Penn State game. Tech fans went to bed past midnight trying to figure out how a 17-0 lead turned into that.

## Eight Straight and Counting the Same Way Nobody Does

Where it stands now. Georgia leads by either count, 73-41-5 or 73-39-5, and has won 8 straight, the longest run in school history. The 8th came last year, 16-9, with No. 4 Georgia beating No. 23 Tech at Mercedes-Benz Stadium.

That was a Tech home game, technically. Tech moved it downtown and Georgia fans appeared to fill at least 60% of the seats. Nothing says home field like most of the building wearing the other guy's color.

::getty{id="2250378265" et="Ray2dm3yTVJ5qeXN1NkdHw" sig="sBrTNyKtu8mxBw3oaCHap4kYgecXTB_pnVWeRZO7zAc=" w="396" h="594" caption="Running back Nate Frazier (3) of the Georgia Bulldogs rushes during the Friday afternoon college football game between the UGA Bulldogs and the Georgia Tech Yel" credit="Icon Sportswire/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/2250378265"}

Tech's last win was 28-27 in 2016. Tech's last home win was 1999, the Sanks game. Most of the students on Tech's campus this fall weren't born the last time Tech beat Georgia in Atlanta, and the team went and gave away its home game anyway.

## Saturday in Athens Looks Like a Mismatch on Paper

Kickoff is Saturday at 3:30 ET in Athens, and the matchup inside the matchup is ugly for Tech.

::getty{id="2304924060" et="o42dq0hYTYxU9qVKUCl3-A" sig="YW1J8FObz1W0ri4jXz78SdTc5QNYykNUVXJ_WdVBJY4=" w="594" h="396" caption="Running back Nate Frazier #3 of the Georgia Bulldogs runs the football during the college football game between the Missouri Tigers and the University of Georgi" credit="Icon Sportswire/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/2304924060"}

Tech's defense allows 213.5 rushing yards a game, 129th in FBS. Georgia runs for 201.6 a game, 20th. A run defense ranked 129th lining up across from a top-20 ground game is a hole you can drive a bus through, and Georgia has a lot of buses.

My sims hate it even more than the stat sheet does. Georgia takes 98 of every 100, the typical sim lands 41-10 Georgia, and half of them finish somewhere between Georgia by 20 and Georgia by 39.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195353.png" alt="Split card in Georgia Tech gold and Georgia red: the sims give Georgia a 98.0% win chance to Tech's 2.0%, a median score of 41-10, and show Tech allowing 213.5 rushing yards a game (129th) while Georgia gains 201.6 (20th)." caption="Georgia wins 98 of every 100 sims, and the matchup on the ground is why." own="1"}

I'm treating that lightly, and I'll tell you why. The last two meetings in this streak were an 8-overtime game and a 16-9 game. Tech has spent a decade losing this game and the last two years losing it close, with a team the stat sheet liked about as much as it likes this one. The sims also don't know anything about 1999 or 2008. Tech wins 2 of every 100 sims, and I'd like one of those two to show up Saturday.

## Four Titles Apiece and Zero Love Lost

Strip away the streaks and the two schools are dead even where it matters most. Tech has four national titles, 1917, 1928, 1952 and 1990. Georgia has four, 1942, 1980, 2021 and 2022. Georgia's two latest are a lot fresher, and Georgia fans will remind you of that, and Tech fans will remind them that Tech had two before Georgia had one.

::photo{src="/blog/clean-old-fashioned-hate-georgia-tech-georgia-rivalry-history/gfx-1126195346.png" alt="A split card headed '4 and 4': a gold Georgia Tech panel with four trophies for its national titles in 1917, 1928, 1952 and 1990, next to a red Georgia panel with four trophies for 1942, 1980, 2021 and 2022." caption="Four national titles apiece: Tech got there first, and Georgia's two are the most recent." own="1"}

Four titles each, 119 games, and they still can't agree on the score. Georgia won't erase the asterisks. Tech won't acknowledge them. The 48-0 either happened or it didn't, depending on which campus you're standing on.

Saturday will probably look like the sims say. It might also look like 2024. Either way, this thing stays hateful whether the game is close or not, and I think both schools would be a little disappointed if it ever got friendly.
