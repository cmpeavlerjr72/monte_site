BYU is No. 2 in the country. Sit with that for a second. Not No. 2 in the Big 12, No. 2 in America, 1,675 poll points and 20 first-place votes, 22 points behind Miami. The Cougars haven't been ranked this high since 1984.

And they're not even the favorite to reach their own conference title game.

Texas Tech is No. 8, Utah is No. 10, Houston is No. 14, and the Big 12 only sends two teams to Arlington. Four contenders, two chairs, and the music starts Saturday night in Salt Lake when Houston walks into Utah's building with GameDay in town.

I'll say it plainly: the Big 12 is the most chaotic conference in America, and this week is the proof.

## Four Teams, Two Chairs, Two Very Different Rankings

The standings are the easy part. Four teams sitting at 3-0 or 3-1 in league play, all four in the AP top 14, two seats in the title game. Somebody good is going home in early December and watching Arlington from the couch.

The hard part is agreeing on who's actually good. The AP poll and our Quads résumé ranking line up on the top two: BYU and Tech, in that order. Then they go their separate ways and don't speak again.

Utah is No. 10 in the AP and 18th in our Quads. That's a 6-0 team that hasn't beaten anybody we'd call a win yet (I say that with love, the defense is the real deal, but go look at the opponents). Houston is the mirror image: No. 14 in the AP, up from 20, and 5th in our Quads. The poll is docking the Cougars for a two-point road loss and crediting Utah for a schedule with nobody on it.

This is what I mean by chaos. The games being close is only half of it. Nobody can even agree on the order of the teams playing them, and the first time BYU and Utah have ever been in the top 10 together, they still have to play each other in two weeks.

So if you make me pick a fraud out of the group, I'm going with Texas Tech, and I'll say upfront that fraud might be harsh. My problem isn't the team. My problem is that Tech is the least challenged team in this race, and that doesn't change between now and the Big 12 championship. BYU and Utah are nowhere on the schedule.

The gripe with Tech is the schedule. The Red Raiders are going to make the championship game without ever needing to prove anything, and the one ranked team they've beaten is Houston, by two, at home. Good team, probably. Tested team, no, and the league isn't going to test them until Arlington.

## Real or Fraud: The Contender Roll Call

BYU: real. You don't beat Notre Dame and jump from No. 8 to No. 2 by accident, and ESPN's strength of record has the Cougars No. 1 in the whole country. Whatever you think of the roster, the résumé is the best in the league and it isn't close.

Texas Tech: good, unproven. Hanging 53 on Arizona State last week is a statement, but the only ranked team the Red Raiders have beaten is Houston, and that one came by two points at home. The AP voter who gave Tech the last first-place vote saw something; I'd like to see it against a team that can punch back.

Utah: the defense is legit and the schedule is a ghost town. 14.8 points allowed a game is elite no matter who you play, and the Utes just went into Boulder and won 27-24. The problem is that Colorado game is about the best thing on the sheet, and it was a field goal. FPI rates Utah ahead of BYU, which tells me FPI likes the talent and hasn't looked at the wins (fair, tho, because there aren't many to look at).

Houston: the best 1-loss team nobody wants to rank in the top 10. Conner Weigman is at 20 touchdowns against 2 interceptions, the offense is putting up 39.3 a game, and the only blemish is a two-point loss on the road to the team ranked eighth in the country. If the Cougars were in the SEC we'd be calling that a quality loss. In the Big 12 it got them a No. 14 and a road trip to Salt Lake to prove it again.

## The Schedule Math: Tech Got the Easy Road

Here's where it gets uncomfortable for the people who want a clean story. Texas Tech doesn't play BYU. Texas Tech doesn't play Utah. The Red Raiders' remaining six are Cincinnati, Arizona, West Virginia, Oklahoma State, Baylor and TCU, and my sims like Tech in every one of them. Not a single game left on the schedule that should scare a top-10 team.

::photo{src="/blog/big-12-chaos-four-contenders-two-title-game-spots/gfx-1021144631.png" alt="Grid of Texas Tech, BYU, Utah and Houston's remaining Big 12 games, each tile shaded red to green by sim win chance: Tech is favored in all six (68% to 94%), BYU's toughest is at Utah (61%), Utah faces a 50% game at Cincinnati and 39% vs BYU, and Houston opens at Utah at 38%." caption="Every game left for the four Big 12 contenders, shaded red (could lose) to green (comfortable) by win chance in my sims." own="1"}

BYU and Utah, meanwhile, have to play each other in Salt Lake on Nov. 7. Utah also gets Houston this week and a trip to Arizona. The two teams with the best defenses in the league are going to beat each other up while Tech jogs.

The math people will push back on me here, and they have a point. ESPN's FPI, in its Week 6 update (before BYU beat Notre Dame, so it's dated), gave three teams a combined 83.3% chance to win the Big 12: Tech 33.6%, Utah 28.0%, BYU 21.7%. By that math it's a three-horse race with a fancy paint job. FPI even gives Utah 55.8% to beat BYU at home.

One Saturday night scrambles every bit of that. Houston wins in Salt Lake and FPI's second horse is suddenly a two-loss team in the league before November. The order can flip on a single kick.

I wanted the actual math, so I simmed every remaining Big 12 game and played the rest of the season out 20,000 times with the Big 12 tiebreakers. Treat these as "in X% of my sims," which is the closest thing I have to a forecast.

Who reaches Arlington in my sims:

| Team | Make Championship | Win Championship |
|---|---|---|
| Texas Tech | 86% | 59% |
| BYU | 66% | 28% |
| Utah | 29% | 9% |
| Houston | 10% | 2% |
| Oklahoma State | 4% | 1% |
| Arizona | 2% | under 1% |
| Baylor | 2% | under 1% |

Tech at 86% is the schedule talking. My sims have the Red Raiders winning each of the six games left more often than not, and the road is so soft that even a loss at Cincinnati on Saturday leaves them in the title game in 71% of my sims. They finish 9-0 or 8-1 in the league in about 7 of every 10 seasons. Rather than that being a showcase of dominance, it's a bye week that lasts six games.

BYU at 66% is the honest number for the best team in the league drawing the hardest path. The biggest game left on anyone's board is BYU at Utah on Nov. 7. The Cougars reach Arlington in 83% of my sims with a win there and 38% with a loss. My sims take BYU in Salt Lake 61% of the time and the middle result is around 27-23, a long way from a comfortable night.

Utah at 29% is the flip side of the same coin, and it's worse than it looks: the Utes' trip to Cincinnati is a dead coin flip in my sims, 50-50, median score 28-28. For a 6-0 top-10 team, that's a lot of ways to lose two.

Houston at Utah. My sims give Utah the win 62% of the time, median 30-24. It's the Weigman offense against a defense giving up 15.2 a game, and I think the Utes get just enough stops. The title-race swing is enormous tho. Houston goes from 2% to 22% to reach Arlington with a win, and Utah goes from 13% to 38%. One game, late at night, with GameDay watching, and a 6-0 team can be functionally out of the race by midnight.

Texas Tech at Cincinnati. Tech wins 68% of my sims, median 31-24. Cincinnati has dropped two straight and the Red Raiders just scored 53. Win and Tech sits at 94% to reach the title game. Lose and, I can't stress this enough, it's still 71%. That is the least pressure any contender in America has on a road game this week.

BYU at UCF. BYU in 79% of my sims, median 28-16. UCF is chasing its first ranked win since it blew out Oklahoma State in 2023, so the building will be loud for a No. 2. BYU is 71% to reach Arlington with a win and 45% with a loss, the gap between "control your fate" and "pray for help in Salt Lake."

The rest of the slate matters too, because in this conference it always does, but those three are the ones that move the seats.

## Two Playoff Teams or One?

This is the one place where quoting the outlets IS the story, because they can't agree and the disagreement is the point.

::photo{src="/blog/big-12-chaos-four-contenders-two-title-game-spots/gfx-1021151741.png" alt="A 12-team playoff bracket built from this week's top 25: byes for No. 1 Miami (ACC champ), No. 2 BYU (Big 12 champ), No. 3 Ohio State (Big Ten champ) and No. 4 Notre Dame; first-round games 8 Texas Tech vs 9 UCLA, 5 Georgia (SEC champ) vs 12 Boise State (No. 21, the G6 rep), 7 Ole Miss vs 10 Utah an" caption="If the playoff started off this week's top 25: three Big 12 teams make it, and Boise State's G6 bid pushes Kentucky out." own="1"}

CBS has BYU as the No. 4 seed and Big 12 champ, with Texas Tech in as the No. 7 seed, and flags that Tech's soft remaining schedule leaves it almost no room for a loss. Yahoo also has two Big 12 teams, BYU at 4 and Tech way down at 11, with Houston and Utah on the fringe. ESPN has BYU in as the No. 3 seed and Utah in with them, and Texas Tech on the wrong side of the bubble.

Three brackets, three different answers on Tech, and ESPN has a 6-0 Utah in a spot where our Quads has the Utes 18th. That's the chaos I'm talking about, and it cuts both ways. The same mess that makes this league the most fun to watch is the thing that could cost it a second bid. Tech's easy road gets it to Arlington and then hands the committee nothing to look at. Utah and BYU knock each other around and one of them wears a loss the committee can't ignore.

I think the league gets two if BYU and Tech both take care of business and meet in Arlington. Any other combination, and we're arguing about whether the Big 12's third-best team deserves it over somebody's second-best.

## Bottom Line: Who's Going to Arlington

Here's where I plant the flag. BYU is in. Not "probably", not "if things break right", in. The Notre Dame game sold me on the Cougars baring a complete and total meltdown.

The second chair is where I zig. I think Utah finds a way into it. Yeah, I spent two sections side-eyeing that résumé, and that defense is still the most real thing on it. Tech has the easy road, the Utes have the harder one and the better excuse to earn it. So my call is the Holy War twice: once in Salt Lake on Nov. 7 and once more in Arlington for the whole thing.

My sims think I've lost it. They have an opinion, and it's a boring one for a chaotic league: BYU vs Texas Tech, a rematch of last year's title game, in **54%** of season simulations. Tech vs Utah is next at 21%, then Houston vs Tech at 7% and my BYU vs Utah dream at 7%.

Tech is the No. 1 seed in 61% of my sims, BYU 27%, Utah 8%. So the most likely picture is the Red Raiders coasting in as the top seed, and the best team in the league by résumé showing up as the 2 after surviving Salt Lake.

That rematch comes with baggage. Last year Tech beat BYU 34-7 in Arlington, in the Cougars' first trip to the game. BYU has spent this season looking like a team that remembers it. Give me the Holy War over the rematch every time, but if you're asking what my sims see, they see those two again, and I don't think the second meeting looks a thing like the first.
