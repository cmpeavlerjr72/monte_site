UCLA is 10-0 and sitting at No. 4, and my sims want to hand it a loss in Ann Arbor to a Michigan team that scores 18 a game. I ran it 10,000 times. I'm not backing off it.

The reason fits in one breath. UCLA lives on the chunk play, Michigan's defense erases chunk plays, and a game with no chunk plays in it at the Big House in late November turns into a coin flip Michigan wins more often than it loses.

Tennessee over LSU is the other game where my sims flip FPI's favorite outright. Behind those two is a pile of underdogs (Navy, Kansas, Vanderbilt, Kansas State) the sims like a lot more than the outside world does, mostly because the sims think the favorites' passing and big-play numbers are puffed up.

## Michigan Can't Score, and It Might Not Need To

Thornton/Getty Images" page="https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/2241876734"}

Michigan takes 54 of every 100 sims at home, and FPI has the Wolverines at 47%. The middle sim lands 20-19 Michigan, so picture a game where every field goal feels like a touchdown.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143341.png" alt="Split card: UCLA averages 5.6 plays of 20+ yards per game on the season, Michigan's defense allows 3.3, and the MVPeav sims project UCLA at 3.459 at Michigan." caption="Michigan's defense pulls UCLA's big-play rate down toward its own number in the sims." own="1"}

UCLA's offense is built on the long one. The Bruins average 5.6 plays of 20+ yards a game on the season, and in my sims against Michigan that drops to about 3.5. Michigan gives up 3.3. The defense is 7th in FBS in yards allowed and 5th in explosive-play rate allowed, and once you take UCLA's explosives away there isn't a ton of offense left behind them.

Because without the big ones, UCLA's offense is 57th in FBS in EPA per play. This is a 10-0 team that beat Oregon 17-15 and Wisconsin 17-7. Great wins, both of them, and both one bad bounce from a loss. The Bruins have been doing it without Nico Iamaleava, who went down for the season at Oregon on Oct. 10. Ty Dieffenbach has started since, left the 22-14 Illinois win for three series with an upper-body injury, came back, and is expected to go Saturday.

Michigan's problems are all on the other side of the ball. The Wolverines score 18.3 points a game, 126th in FBS, and sit at -10 in turnover margin while UCLA is +16, the best in the country. Whittingham fired OC Jason Beck after the Michigan State loss and still hasn't named a starting quarterback. Underwood and Carr have 9 interceptions against 7 touchdowns between them, and JJ Buchanan, the top receiver at 646 yards, is probably done for the year.

So Michigan may need 20 points to win this and it hasn't shown it can get to 20 against a sidewalk. The sims say 20 is enough more than half the time, because UCLA stops getting the free points it's been living on.

## Tennessee Takes Away LSU's Third Downs

Tennessee is No. 9 and FPI still has this leaning LSU by a hair, 49.9% for the Vols. My sims have Tennessee at 53%, and the typical sim is a 22-21 Vols win. Nobody's running away with this one.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143413.png" alt="Slope chart: LSU's third-down conversion rate falls from 52% on the season to 34.3% in the sims against Tennessee, a -17.7 point drop toward the 32.7% Tennessee allows, alongside a note that Sam Leavitt has 12 interceptions, 3 in last week's loss to Texas." caption="LSU converts 52% of third downs on the season; in my sims against Tennessee that falls to 34.3%, close to the 32.7% the Vols allow." own="1"}

The mechanism is third down. LSU converts 52% on the season, which is elite. In my sims against Tennessee that falls to 34.3%, and Tennessee allows 32.7% on the year. The drop is what happens when a pass defense ranked 8th in FBS in yards per attempt allowed and 9th in EPA per play meets a quarterback who already has 12 interceptions. Sam Leavitt threw 3 of them in last week's loss to Texas.

Tennessee is +8 in turnover margin, 6th in the country, and takeaways are the cheapest points an offense this limited is going to find. Which matters, because LSU's defense is 5th in FBS in yards allowed and 3rd in EPA per play. Tennessee's passing offense is 129th in yards per game and keeps losing bodies, with WR Mike Matthews and RB Daune Morris out for the season and LG Nic Moore increasingly unlikely to play. George MacIntyre is working toward playing on the knee, and the starter is still unsettled.

So the Vols are trying to win a game in the low 20s with a quarterback question of their own, against a defense that doesn't give up much of anything. My sims say LSU's offense cracks first.

## Navy Runs Right at the 127th Run Defense on Senior Day

Memphis is No. 24 and tied for first in the American, and FPI gives Navy a 36.7% shot in Annapolis. My sims give the Mids 48%, and the typical sim is Memphis 28-27. A toss-up on senior day, with Navy needing help just to sniff a bowl.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143341.png" alt="Split card of Navy's offense against Memphis' defense: Navy runs for 298.1 yards per game (3rd) while Memphis allows 197.2 (127th) and 5.0 yards per carry (121st), and the median sim score is Memphis 28, Navy 27." caption="Navy's option meets one of the worst run defenses in FBS, and the sims still call it a one-point game." own="1"}

This is the one I'd watch for the football. Navy runs for 298 yards a game, 3rd in FBS, and is No. 1 in the country in EPA per play. Memphis gives up 197 rushing yards a game, 127th of 138 teams. Jackson Gutierrez alone has 1,159 rushing yards and 18 touchdowns. Memphis knows exactly what's coming and hasn't shown anyone it can stop it.

The sims also take a bite out of the Memphis passing game. Marcus Stokes has 2,845 yards and 28 touchdowns, and the Tigers come into the sim at 300 passing yards a game; against Navy my sims bring that down to 238.

Navy is 3-6, though, and it earned the record. It lost at FAU, UAB, Air Force and UTSA, and gave up 49 at home to North Texas in a 39-49 loss. The Mids are 119th in turnover margin at -6, and the pass defense allows 9.2 yards per attempt, 136th in FBS, which is the worst possible thing to carry into a game against Stokes. Hold the ball and keep Memphis on the sideline, Navy wins. Put the option on the ground twice and Stokes gets his numbers anyway.

## Kansas Lives in One-Score Games, and BYU Is Next

BYU is No. 12 with its Big 12 title hopes riding on this one, and FPI gives Kansas 29%. My sims give the Jayhawks 42%, and the middle sim is BYU 27-24. Which is right where Kansas has spent its whole season.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143339.png" alt="Split card: Kansas, 17th in explosive-play rate, averages 6.4 plays of 20+ yards per game but drops to 4.4 against BYU in MVPeav's sims, while BYU's defense, 6th in explosive rate allowed, gives up 3.6 per game." caption="Kansas' big-play offense gets trimmed by BYU's defense, but it doesn't go away." own="1"}

Kansas is 4-6 and three of its games fit on one line. Beat Baylor 25-24, lost 32-33 to UCF, lost 16-22 at TCU. Flip two of those and this is a bowl team already instead of one that needs wins just to get to six.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143350.png" alt="Left-to-right timeline of Kansas' season: beat Baylor 25-24 (Oct 24), lost 16-22 at TCU (Oct 31), lost 32-33 to UCF (Nov 7), then Isaiah Marshall ran for a season-high 164 against West Virginia (Nov 14)." caption="Kansas has spent its season in one-score games, and Isaiah Marshall's 164-yard day shows why the offense still scares people." own="1"}

The offense is why the sims take it seriously. Kansas is 17th in explosive-play rate and 18th in rush yards per game, and Isaiah Marshall just ran for a season-high 164 against West Virginia. BYU's defense is 6th in explosive rate allowed, and the sims respect it. Kansas' 6.4 chunk plays a game shrink to about 4.4 in this one, and BYU allows 3.6. Even trimmed, that's still a dangerous offense.

The worry is the ball. Kansas is -5 in turnover margin, 112th, and Dylan Edwards is uncertain with an ankle. On the other side, BYU's offense is only 59th in points per game, which is why the sims keep this one tight even after they cut Kansas down to size.

## Vanderbilt and Kansas State Are the Long Shots

Two shakier cases, quick.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143347.png" alt="Vanderbilt wins 33.9% of 10,000 sims at Florida versus ESPN FPI's 15.3%, with a median sim score of Florida 28, Vanderbilt 22 and Florida's defense 113th in success rate allowed (44%)." caption="The biggest split on the board: my sims give Vanderbilt more than twice FPI's chance in Gainesville." own="1"}

Vanderbilt at Florida is the biggest split between my sims and FPI on the whole board. FPI gives Vandy 15.3%, my sims give it 34%, and the typical sim is Florida 28-22. Florida is No. 23 and has the wins, 27-24 at Texas and 52-28 over Ole Miss, but its defense is 113th in success rate allowed at 44% and 95th in pass yards allowed per game. Soft is the word.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143358.png" alt="Kansas State wins 37.1% of mvpeav sims against No. 19 Arizona versus ESPN FPI's 25.5%, as the sims cut Arizona's passing from a 343.3-yard season average to 265.0 against K-State, with Arizona's run game 111th in FBS." caption="Arizona throws for 343.3 yards a game, but only 265.0 in my sims against K-State. That's enough to push Kansas State's upset chance to 37.1%." own="1"}

The sims don't even punish Florida's offense much. The Gators' interceptions go from 1.0 a game to about 0.7 against Vandy's defense, and the run game stays near 4.9 a carry. Vandy's whole case is Florida's defense, and Vandy's whole problem is it can't run. 102 rush yards a game, 129th, at 3.1 a carry, 133rd, with an offense 112th in success rate. Vanderbilt is 4-6 and just lost 6-21 to Alabama at home. One in three feels about right for an offense that gets stuffed on first down.

Kansas State gets a look because No. 19 Arizona is a one-trick offense. The Wildcats are 3rd in FBS in pass yards per game but 111th in rushing and 109th in yards per carry, and the sims cut Arizona's passing from 343 a game to 265 against a K-State defense that comes in allowing 197. That alone gets Kansas State to 37% in my sims, against FPI's 25.5%, with a typical sim of Arizona 28-22.

Everything else about K-State is a mess. It's 4-6, 1-6 in the Big 12, on its longest losing streak since 2020, and both Avery Johnson and backup Jacob Knuth are reported out. The defense is 129th in explosive-play rate allowed at 14% and 97th against the run, which is at least the one thing Arizona doesn't do. A team with no quarterback and a defense that gives up explosives against the best passing game in the league is a long shot for a reason.

## The One Upset the Sims Like Most

Line up all six and my sims rank them like this. Michigan 54%, Tennessee 53%, Navy 48%, Kansas 42%, Kansas State 37%, Vanderbilt 34%. Only the top two come out as outright sim favorites, and only the top one involves an unbeaten team ranked 4th in the country.

::photo{src="/blog/upset-watch-week-12-sims-michigan-ucla-navy-memphis/gfx-1118143346.png" alt="Logo ladder of how often ESPN FPI's Week 12 underdogs win in MVPeav's sims: Michigan 53.6% vs No. 4 UCLA, Tennessee 53.3% vs LSU, Navy 47.8% vs No. 24 Memphis, Kansas 42.1% vs No. 12 BYU, Kansas State 37.1% vs No. 19 Arizona, Vanderbilt 33.9% at No. 23 Florida." caption="Only Michigan and Tennessee come out as outright sim favorites among FPI's Week 12 underdogs." own="1"}

Michigan over UCLA is the call. A defense that erases exactly the thing UCLA does best, a home crowd in late November, and a Bruins offense that sits 57th in EPA per play once the long ones stop coming. Michigan has to find 20 points with no quarterback and no top receiver, and the sims think it does, barely, 54 times out of 100.

Navy is the one to actually sit down and watch. 298 yards of option a game into the 127th run defense in the country, on senior day, with the Mids needing every bit of it. If the ball stays off the turf, Memphis is in trouble.
